| 83 | How the SCIM token is made, kept, rotated and revoked, and what SCIM takes | Issue #152: "behind a bearer token for the IdP's provisioning, hashed like API tokens"; SCIM deactivates people and decides their groups, platform admins' included, so its credential is as strong as the strongest role | **A credential of its own, never in Quai's hands**: `quai scim-token` prints `quai_scim_` and 64 hex characters (256 random bits) and its SHA-256; the configuration names the secret holding the hash (`auth.scim.tokens: [{name, sha256: {ref}}]`, one to four, two while rotating), so the running Quai cannot mint or show one, and no admin API exists to steal one through. Compared in constant time with each token's hash; one `Authorization` header, `Bearer`, the prefix, at most 128 bytes; an unreadable secret is 503, never a pass. Valid on `/scim/v2` only, which takes no session or API token. Revoked by removing the entry or changing its secret (cached a minute); last use recorded (to the minute) and shown in the access report; every change attributed in the audit log to `scim:<name>`. Rate limited (50/s, burst 100) before any work; bounds on the body (1 MiB), media type, objects, strings, operations, members and pages; a strict filter parser to query parameters. Option: a token made by an admin API with step-up (Quai would then hold the means to make one) | GA |
| 84 | How SCIM binds a provisioned person to their account of the identity provider, and what a rename or a reused name does | A person is their name everywhere in Quai; SCIM sends a `userName` (the UPN, which changes and is reused) and an `externalId`; binding on a name would let a new employee given an old UPN act as their predecessor, or a provisioned record take over someone's account | **By `externalId`, which must be the immutable identifier (Entra ID's `oid`, or `sub`) and never changes (400 `mutability`)**. A new user's `userName` becomes their Quai name, for good; a rename changes only the `userName`. Someone who signed in before is adopted only when their stored `oid` (else `sub`) is the `externalId`; another account's name, a name equal but for case, another user's `externalId` or a reserved name is 409 `uniqueness`, at creation and rename. At sign-in, a provisioned person is found by `oid` (else `sub`) and acts as SCIM's name; before their first sign-in the ID token's name must be that name or the current `userName`; a provisioned name with another identifier, or a provisioned identifier with another name, is refused. DELETE deactivates, removes memberships and unbinds: the row and name stay (a new person with that `userName` is refused, inheriting nothing), and the same `externalId` provisioned again re-adopts it. An administrator can release a name deliberately (operations guide). Options: identify people by `oid` everywhere (question 45's option) | GA |
| 85 | How provisioned groups and the ID token's groups combine, and when a change takes effect | Groups drive roles, platform admin included; with two sources, one could grant what the other withholds, and removing someone from the admin group must not wait for their session to end | **SCIM only takes away**: a provisioned person's groups are their last sign-in's, less each group SCIM has provisioned (its name, by `auth.scim.groupName`: `externalId` by default, or `displayName`; names are remembered after a rename or delete) that does not list them. A group added by SCIM but absent from the ID token is not held. Sessions and API tokens act with the groups they were issued with that the person still holds, checked at every request: removals apply at once, additions to credentials issued with the group, otherwise at the next sign-in. Each change is audited with groups and grants before and after (`auth.groups_changed`). People SCIM does not provision keep their ID token's groups. Options: SCIM authoritative for groups (it grants); revoking sessions on any group change | GA |
| 86 | What a deactivation by SCIM does, and what a reactivation restores | Issue #152: "at once, the person's sessions and API tokens are revoked, their pending requests cancelled, and their pending approvals dropped with the approvers notified again" | **At once and completely**, in the request that deactivates (`active: false`, `"False"` or a DELETE): the session epoch moves, so every session ends at its next request; API tokens revoked; delegations given or received revoked; requests not yet delivered cancelled ("its requester was deactivated"); their reviews, and reviews on their behalf, of requests still pending approval voided and the approvers notified again (`reviews_voided`); each an audit event of the provisioning. Approvals of requests already approved or delivered stand, as delivery may be under way. A request whose manager approver is deactivated waits until it expires. Chat links fail (they need the session); notifications to them are skipped; sign-ins refused. **Reactivation restores nothing** revoked: the person signs in afresh. SCIM is authoritative for `active`: it reactivates even a manual deactivation. Options: void approvals of approved, undelivered requests too; re-plan requests whose manager left | GA |
| 87 | Where a provisioned person's manager comes from | Issue #152: "the enterprise extension's `manager` feeds `manager` approvers"; a claim or Graph could disagree with SCIM | **SCIM alone, for the people it provisions**: the enterprise extension's `manager.value`, the manager's SCIM `id` (a UUID, checked), resolved at planning to that provisioned person; one not provisioned yet, deactivated, or the requester is no manager, with the reason. Sign-ins keep it, whatever `auth.oidc.managerClaim` or `auth.graph.manager` say; others keep those sources. A change is audited (`scim.user_changed`, as pseudonyms). Option: let a sign-in's source override SCIM's | GA |
| 88 | What is out of SCIM's reach, and how much of SCIM Quai implements | Emergency accounts must sign in whatever provisioning does; a provisioning bug or a leaked token must not lock out the last way in; minimizing the personal data kept | **`auth.scim.reserved`** (the identity provider's emergency, break-glass accounts) **and the development users' names** are never created or renamed to (409), changed, deactivated or deleted (403), in any case. The `break-glass` role is a group's like any other: a provisioned leaver loses it. **Kept:** `userName`, `externalId`, `active`, the enterprise `manager`; groups' `displayName` (unique), `externalId` (set once) and members. Everything else (emails, names, roles) is accepted in Entra ID's forms and dropped. **Implemented:** GET (one, list with `eq`/`and` filters and pages of at most 200), POST, PATCH (add, replace, remove; paths with or without filters as Entra sends them; members removed by a value filter, or all), DELETE, and the discovery endpoints; PUT and Bulk are 501; no sorting or ETags; replacing a group's members is refused. Outside Huma, since SCIM has its own media type and error format. Option: PUT, for other identity providers | GA |
| 89 | How Quai's and a repository's change windows combine, and which windows a merge sees | Issue #153: windows in the configuration and the repository document; a repository's admins must not lift a global freeze, and a freeze added after an approval must hold the request | **Every window that applies must be open**, Quai's configuration's and the repository document's alike: a repository's windows only add constraints (freezes, narrower hours), never widen or lift the configuration's. **Decided at merge time**, under the request's row lock and again under its merge lock before Quai asks the platform, with the windows as stored then (the repository document read from the database when the registry has not reloaded it), never as they were at approval. A repository document whose windows do not compile is refused; one stored that Quai cannot read holds its requests. Option: windows that open (an exception to a freeze), with an approval of their own | GA |
| 90 | How a window's times are read: syntax, zones, daylight saving, overlaps, how far ahead | A wall-clock window is ambiguous on the days the clock jumps, and the next opening must be computable and bounded | **`<days> HH:MM-HH:MM <zone>`** (days `Mon`–`Sun`, lists and wrapping ranges; `24:00` ends a day; an entry does not cross midnight) and **`<from> to <to> <zone>`** (minutes or dates, from before to); IANA zone names only, checked at load, from the system's database or the one built into Quai. **Wall-clock rule**: an allow entry holds while the zone's clock reads inside it, so a skipped hour never comes and a repeated one comes twice; a freeze runs from the first reading of its start to the last reading of its end (a time in a skipped hour is the jump): the longer freeze when ambiguous. Allow entries add up; freezes add up and win. The next opening and closing are looked for within **366 days**; none means the request waits, its page and `quai/approval` saying none opens within a year. At most 32 windows per source and 32 entries per window. Options: recurring freezes (every 24 December), windows named in a type | GA |
| 91 | What a change window's `when` sees, and what a condition that fails does | Issue #153: `when` conditions run with the request's context only; a requester must not choose when their change merges, and an error must never read as "in window" | **`params` and `request`** (`kind`, `action`, `type`, `blueprint`, `repository`), for each of the request's resources as planned (kept with the request, `window_subjects`): the window applies when it holds for one. Not `inputs`, `doc`, `previous`, `action`, `user`, `plan`, nor the request's reason (empty there). **A condition that fails holds the request** whatever the time, with the error on its page and in `quai/approval`; it goes on once the condition is fixed. A request planned before P4-16 has no resources recorded: every window with a `when` applies to it | GA |
| 92 | Whether an approved request waiting for its change window expires | P4-14's expiry is for pending approval; a long freeze can keep an approved request waiting for weeks | **No**: it waits, approved, until its windows open, it is cancelled, or it goes stale (its files or definitions changed). Option: an expiry for approved requests too, counted from the approval | GA |
| 93 | What happens to a merge already asked of the platform when a window closes, and to a merge made on the platform while Quai holds a request | Auto-merge, auto-complete and a merge queue merge on their own once the checks pass, and a person with the rights can merge regardless of a check | **Quai turns `quai/approval` pending within a minute of the window closing** (the windows' sweep, every minute), which stops an armed merge as a required check; a merging request goes back to approved under its merge lock. A merge already under way (a merge group GitHub approved, checks passing in that minute) can land just after the close: the residual. **A merge on the platform while Quai holds the request** is a bypass: applied as merged, `merged_outside_window` (event and audit), the bypass alert to the admins. A merge Quai learns of after a window closed, made before Quai held the request, is not one. Options: disarm at close (disable auto-merge, leave the queue, cancel auto-complete); judge a merge by the platform's merge time | GA |
